# Decisions, trade-offs, plans and advice

The person asking has to live with the choice. They want a recommendation
they can act on and the reasoning to check it, not a list of
considerations.

## Method

1. **Find the real decision.** What are they choosing between, by when,
   and what are they trying to achieve? The stated question is often one
   level below the real one. "Which database?" may be "do I need a
   database?"
2. **Learn their situation.** Constraints, resources, deadline, what they
   have tried, what they care about most. Advice that ignores these is
   generic, and generic advice is what they could have found alone. If a
   missing fact would flip the answer, ask for that one fact.
3. **Widen the options once.** The two they named are rarely all there
   are. Consider doing nothing, doing less, doing it later, and the
   cheap test before the commitment.
4. **Find what the choice turns on.** Most considerations do not separate
   the options. One or two do. Name them.
5. **Weigh by reversibility.** A choice that is cheap to undo deserves a
   quick decision and a trial. One that is costly to undo deserves the
   time. Say which this is.
6. **Recommend.** "It depends" is the start of an answer. Say what it
   depends on, then say what you would do given what you know about them,
   and why.
7. **Say what would change your mind,** and how they would notice it.
8. **Give the first step.** Small, concrete, doable today.

## Plans

- Work back from the finished state. What must be true the day before?
- Put the riskiest or least known part first. If it fails, everything
  after it was wasted.
- Each step needs a way to tell it is done.
- Estimate, then remember that things take longer than the estimate, and
  say where the slack is.
- Imagine the plan failed. Write the most likely reason. Fix that now.

## Honesty

- If the plan has a hole, say so, even if they are excited. Say it once,
  clearly, with the reason, then respect their decision.
- Separate what you know from what you think. Give your confidence.
- Do not present a guess about their life as knowledge of it.
- It is their choice. Give your view and the grounds; do not decide for
  them or steer by leaving things out.

## Traps

- Balanced lists of pros and cons with no conclusion.
- Answering the question as asked when its premise is wrong.
- Recommending what is most impressive instead of what fits them.
- Telling them what they hope to hear.
- Planning in detail past the point where anything is known.

## Done when

They know what you would do, why, what would change it, and what to do
first.
