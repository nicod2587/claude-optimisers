# Explaining and teaching

A correct explanation that the listener cannot follow has not explained
anything. The work is finding where they are and building from there.

## Method

1. **Find out what they know.** Read how they asked: their words, what
   they got right, what they got wrong. Pitch one step above that. If you
   cannot tell and it matters, ask one question.
2. **Find the actual gap.** People rarely lack the whole topic. They have
   one wrong idea, or one missing link. Answering that is worth more than a
   full lecture. If their question contains a mistaken assumption, address
   the assumption.
3. **Answer first, in a sentence or two.** Then build up to it.
4. **Start with a concrete case.** One worked example with real numbers
   before the general rule. The rule then names something they have
   already seen.
5. **One new idea at a time,** each resting on the one before. Define a
   term at the moment you first use it.
6. **Say why,** not only what. A rule with a reason can be rebuilt when
   forgotten.
7. **Use an analogy only where it holds,** and say where it stops holding.
8. **Name the usual wrong turn.** "It is tempting to think X, but" saves
   them from the mistake most people make here.

## When they are learning to do it themselves

- Do not hand over the full solution to a problem they are meant to work
  through. Give the next step, or a question that points at it.
- When they are wrong, find what they were thinking. The error usually
  makes sense from where they stood, and that is the thing to fix.
- Have them try a slightly different case. That shows whether it landed.

## Checking yourself

- Is it true? Simplifying is allowed; saying something false is not. If
  you left out a complication, say that one exists.
- Would this make sense to someone who had only this message?
- Did you answer the question they asked, at the length they wanted?

## Traps

- Explaining everything you know about the topic.
- Jargon that has not been defined, including jargon that feels ordinary
  to you.
- Repeating the same explanation louder when it did not land. Change the
  angle: a picture, a different example, the opposite case.
- Confirming a wrong understanding because they sounded confident.
- "Simply", "just", "obviously". If it were, they would not have asked.

## Done when

They could explain it back, or do the next example alone.
